2 · Ground contrast

Geometry held at B. The question is how hard the white cards separate from the plum — too little and they glare, too much and the slice looks pasted on.

Quiet — flat #241d3d, dots at .05, no pool. Maximum separation.
Pooled — dots .07 over a soft light pool. Echoes the hero.
Lifted — #35294f base, stronger pool. Warmer, less contrast.
What to look for. At 590px the $XX B numeral, all three card titles and the chart's three lines hold. The table rows below become texture — that is expected and fine. The thing to actually judge is whether the slice reads as a fragment of something larger (which is the claim) or as a screenshot someone pasted on a purple rectangle.

Why the run-off goes right, not left. The first mock ran the slice off the left page edge. It failed: $XX B and every card title sit hard against the slice's left edge, so the numeral rendered as "B" and the titles as "…ty Planning Timeline". Running off the bottom-right instead keeps the left column whole and points the fragment into the page.

Known gap. .csimg does not currently bleed off the page edge in v15 — .worklist is a plain centred 1180px with no negative margin, so these render as clean boxes.
