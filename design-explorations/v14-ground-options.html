v14 — full-width ground, hip down

Four readings of “something faint across the full width, behind me, hip to bottom.” No panels, no edges, nothing above 52% of the hero. Each one shows the metric cards so you can judge the competition directly.

What I changed in my thinking

The panel was boxy because it was a shape with an edge — and a vertical edge next to a grid of cards will always look like a fifth card. Everything below is a gradient with no edge anywhere, spanning the full page width.

On the geometry: your hip lands at about 66% of the hero's height, and the metric cards cut into the hero at about 85%. So the usable zone is a band between those two lines — which is narrow, and is why option d exists: it's the only one that clears out completely before the cards begin.

How I'd read these

c is where I'd go. It's the only one that doesn't read as a horizontal band, because it's weighted to her rather than spread evenly — it looks like light pooling behind her, and it's gone by the time it reaches the left and right page edges, so there's no line for the cards to argue with.

d is the safest if the cards still feel crowded. Nothing at all touches the card line.

b is the riskiest — a horizon line is another horizontal edge, which is close to what didn't work last time. In there because a literal line is one honest reading of "something across the width," not because I expect it to win.

All four are faint by design, and screen brightness affects them a lot. If the direction is right but the strength is wrong, that's one number — say “c but twice as strong” and it's done.
