v13 — quieter backdrops

The contours are out. Six alternatives, ordered from nothing at all to the most texture I'd still be comfortable putting behind a face. Everything else on the page is v12 as built.

What I think the backdrop is actually for

The contours failed because they were lines, and lines behind a face read as competition — your eye keeps trying to resolve them. Density wasn't the problem; the medium was.

Which reframes the job. The hero's real weakness isn't that it's empty — it's that the portrait is a cut-out standing on nothing. She floats, and then stops. So the useful backdrop is one that grounds her, not one that decorates the space. That's why the first four options below are all tone and no pattern.

How I'd read these

iii — tonal panel is where I'd land. It's the only one that does structural work: it separates portrait from message, gives the black outfit something to sit against instead of dissolving into white, and it costs one gradient. It also scales — the same panel edge can reappear on the case study pages.

ii — grounding shadow if you want the page to stay essentially white. It fixes the floating problem and nothing else, which may be exactly right.

These combine, incidentally: iii + ii together gives a panel to sit against and weight at her feet. Say the word and I'll show that pairing.

Fair warning on vi — it's the closest to what you already rejected. Masked or not, it's still a pattern behind a face. I've included it because it's the option that keeps continuity with v11, not because I think it wins.
